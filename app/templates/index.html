Understanding Cancer

Essential clinical insights on cancer biology, warning signs, and early detection

What is Cancer?

Cancer arises when normal cells undergo genetic mutations, leading to uncontrolled cellular growth, division, and tissue invasion. While healthy cells follow a strict lifecycle of growth and death, cancerous cells ignore cellular signals, forming abnormal masses or tumors.

Benign Tumors Non-cancerous, localized, and do not spread to surrounding tissues.
Malignant Tumors Cancerous, invasive, and capable of metastasizing to distant organs.

7 Early Warning Signs

  • Unexplained weight loss or persistent fatigue
  • Persistent cough or hoarseness (Lung indicator)
  • Skin mole changes in color, size, or shape (Skin indicator)
  • Persistent headaches, seizures, or vision changes (Brain indicator)
  • Lumps or tissue thickening in breast, neck, or soft tissue
  • Unexplained bleeding or unusual body discharges
  • Non-healing sores or persistent indigestion

Key Challenges in Cancer Care

Systemic hurdles faced by patients and regional health networks in Erode District

Diagnostic Delays

Over 65% of cancer patients in regional districts present at Stage III or IV due to absent routine screening, symptom misinterpretation, and referral delays.

Rural Access Deficit

Specialized oncologists and DICOM imaging facilities are heavily centralized in major tier-1 cities, forcing rural patients to travel long distances for care.

Financial Toxicity

High out-of-pocket costs for advanced molecular imaging and multi-stage biopsy procedures often lead to treatment abandonment in low-income households.
